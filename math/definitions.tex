\section{Machine Learning}

\subsection{inductive bias}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Restricting the hypothesis space $\mathcal{H}$ to a certain set of
  hypotheses.

  Smaller $\mathcal{H}$ means less data needed to pin down the right
  hypothesis $h$.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{example: symmetry as inductive bias}

A CNN restricts $\mathcal{H}$ from all functions $\mathbb{R}^{d \times d}
\to \{0, 1\}$ to just the translation-equivariant ones,
\[
  \mathcal{H} = \{ f : f(\tau_a x) = \tau_a\, f(x) \quad \forall a \},
\]
where $\tau_a$ translates the image by $a$. The network never has to spend
training data learning that an edge is an edge at every position, since the
hypothesis cannot express ``not an edge at position 37'' --- that is why it
generalizes.
